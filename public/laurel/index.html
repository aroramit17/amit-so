The situation · week one

What I walked into.

  • Salesforce has incomplete data. Missing and duplicate account records across multiple business units. No clear logic around account ownership or pool management.
  • Duplicate fields with varying levels of accuracy. Self-serve reporting is impossible.
  • Outreach and Salesforce aren't syncing consistently. Some activities log. Some don't.
  • The CX team is manually tracking pipeline stages in a spreadsheet because they don't trust Salesforce data.
  • No documentation exists for any current workflow or integration.
The deliverable

What I'm handing back.

  1. 01

    A prioritized 30-day action plan.

    What I tackle first, why, and how I approach it.

  2. 02

    A one-page runbook.

    For the Outreach ↔ Salesforce sync issue. Written so a new admin joining the team can troubleshoot from it.

  3. 03

    How I'd use AI to move faster.

Part one I. Prioritized action plan

Thirty days.
Four weeks.

The instinct walking into this is to start fixing things. That's the trap. With this much undocumented chaos, anything I "fix" before I understand the system breaks something I can't see. The plan below front-loads listening, stops the one active bleed, then moves to design and controlled cleanup. In that order. Click a week for the moves.

Week 01 · Discovery

Leave the week with a clear picture of what exists, who owns it, and what's actively breaking.

The interviews build political capital. The metadata snapshot is the evidence base for every decision that follows. The sync error monitor quantifies the damage while I work out the fix.

01 ·

Stakeholder interviews

30 minutes each with VP Sales, VP CX, Marketing Ops, RevOps lead, Finance, and 2–3 frontline reps per team. Same five questions every time. What data do you trust. What don't you. Walk me through your day. What wastes your time. What would you fix first.

02 ·

Access & system audit

Full admin in SFDC, Outreach, and every connected system. Pull the Connected Apps list, Installed Packages, and OAuth tokens in SFDC. This alone usually surfaces integrations nobody remembers.

03 ·

Metadata snapshot

Export all metadata via SFDX (or Gearset / Salto if licensed): fields, validation rules, flows, processes, Apex, profiles, permission sets. The evidence base for every decision that follows.

04 ·

Field usage analysis

Use a free AppExchange tool like Field Pro to scan Account, Contact, Opportunity, Lead. Which custom fields are populated. Which are dead weight. (Or pull FieldUsage from the SFDC API directly if Field Trip isn't installed.) Critical input for week 3.

05 ·

Apollo Outreach sync triage

Open the sync logs, find the actual failure mode, decide if it's a same-day or same-week fix. Even if root cause takes longer, get a daily sync-error report landing in my inbox so I know if it's getting worse.

06 ·

Shadow IT inventory

The CX spreadsheet is one of these. Find the rest. There are always more.

Week 02 · Stabilization

Fix the sync. Earn the right to touch anything else.

Every day the Outreach sync is broken is another reason the team won't trust the system when it's time to make real changes. The change freeze is non-negotiable for anything downstream.

01 ·

Fix the Outreach ↔ SFDC sync

Detail in Part II. Non-negotiable.

02 ·

Sync health dashboard

Activity counts by user by day in both systems, side-by-side. The moment they diverge, I know before the users complain.

03 ·

CX deep-dive

Sit with two CX reps for half a day each. Watch them work. The spreadsheet tells me what SFDC is missing. Every column they're tracking maps to a field I need to add, surface, or fix. Tag each column: exists, wrong, missing, duplicate.

04 ·

Duplicate scan, report-only

Native SFDC duplicate and matching rules turned on but not blocking. Get a number. 12% dupes, 30% dupes. The scale dictates the cleanup strategy.

05 ·

Change freeze proposal

Present to leadership. A 30-day soft freeze on new fields and automations. Nothing goes in without my review. Non-negotiable for anything downstream.

Week 03 · Design

Write the things nobody wrote down.

Ownership, fields, integrations, runbooks. The artifacts a successor (or me, in six months) will actually use.

01 ·

Account ownership model

With VP Sales. How accounts get assigned. How pools work. What triggers reassignment. The rule for unassigned accounts. Documented. The single biggest unblocker for everything else.

02 ·

Field consolidation plan

From the usage data, identify duplicate field clusters (Industry__c, Industry_v2__c, Account_Industry__c). Pick the survivor, define migration logic, deprecate the rest. One page per object.

03 ·

Integration map

Every system touching SFDC, sync direction, objects/fields, credential owner, doc location.

04 ·

Top 5 workflow runbooks

Outreach sync, lead routing, opp stage progression, account ownership transfer, MQL → SAL handoff.

05 ·

CX dashboard v1

Rebuild what they're tracking in the spreadsheet as a real SFDC dashboard. Pilot with one rep.

Week 04 · First cuts

Make one change. Watch what breaks. Then scale.

The change-management muscle gets built on the smallest possible task. Then the 30-day retro. What I found. What I fixed. What I need for the next sixty.

01 ·

First field deprecation

One field. Communicate it. Watch what breaks. Build the change-management muscle on something low-stakes.

02 ·

Pilot duplicate account merge

50–100 records, manual review, sales sign-off on each. Prove the playbook before scaling.

03 ·

CX pilot rep off the spreadsheet

Migrate one rep entirely to the SFDC dashboard. If it works for one, scale to the team in month two.

04 ·

30-day retro with leadership

What I found. What I fixed. The 60- and 90-day roadmap. What I need: budget for tooling (Salto, Gearset, Validity) and executive air cover for the change freeze.

Part two II. The runbook

Outreach ↔ Salesforce
sync troubleshooting.

Purpose: diagnose and resolve cases where Outreach activities (calls, emails, meetings, sequences) aren't appearing in Salesforce, or vice versa. Owned by GTM Systems. Escalate to vendor only after steps 1–6. Each step expands.

Note on provenance: I haven't run Outreach in production. I built and ran this exact playbook against Apollo.io's Salesforce sync at webAI. The menu paths and error messages below are reconciled against Outreach's official documentation; the diagnostic order — logs → user mapping → sync settings → SFDC side → locking → verify — is vendor-agnostic.

PurposeActivity sync diagnosis
OwnerGTM Systems
Escalate afterSteps 1–6 / 2 hours
Before I startOutreach admin, SFDC admin, one example

Confirm the failure.
Get a specific example. "It's not syncing" is not actionable. You need user + record ID + activity type + timestamp. Without a concrete case, stop and ask.

Read the Outreach sync logs.
Outreach → Settings → Integrations → Salesforce → Sync Activity. Filter to user and date.
"Success" → issue is on the SFDC side. Skip to step 5.
"Failed" with error → match to the error table below.
No record at all → Outreach didn't try. Go to step 3.

Verify the Plugin Authentication User.
Outreach connects through a single "Plugin Authentication User" in Salesforce. Every sync runs as that user. If their permissions break, everyone's sync breaks. Check:
  • User active in SFDC
  • Email on the Outreach user matches the SFDC User record
  • Profile has API Enabled
  • Profile has Modify All Data or at least full edit on every mapped field, every relevant Record Type, and every related object referenced by Look-up / Master-Detail fields
Fix, then trigger a manual sync on one test record and confirm.

Audit the sync settings.
Outreach → Settings → Integrations → Salesforce → Plugin Settings. Confirm:
  • Activity type (call / email / meeting) is enabled for sync
  • Object (Contact vs. Lead vs. Account) matches where the record lives
  • Field mappings are intact. A renamed or deleted SFDC field silently breaks sync. Tell-tale sign: a raw API name like Sales_Region__c shows up in the mapping list instead of the friendly label
  • No Formula fields in the mapping. Outreach can't sync them. Supported types: Text, Number, Picklist, URL, Phone, Date, Checkbox
  • "Look for name instead of record ID" is only supported on Lead Status and Opportunity Stage. If it's toggled on for anything else (Accounts, custom picklists), the sync will fail

Cross to the Salesforce side.
  • Field-Level Security: the Plugin Authentication User's profile must have Edit on every field Outreach writes to (Task, Event, Contact, Lead). One missing FLS breaks the whole record.
  • Record Type access: profile must include the Record Type the record is being written against.
  • Validation rules: a rule expecting a value Outreach doesn't send blocks the write. Reproduce by manually creating the record in SFDC with the same payload.
  • Required fields: Outreach won't fill custom required fields.
  • Apex triggers: check active triggers on Task / Event. An exception blocks the insert silently from Outreach's view. The Plugin Authentication User also needs access to the Apex class itself — trigger access alone isn't enough.
  • Workflow emails: if a workflow fires "Send Email" on save, the Plugin Authentication User needs Send Email permission and access to the email folder the template lives in.
  • API limits: Setup → System Overview → API Usage. If exhausted, sync fails.

Look for record locking.
On high-traffic objects, look for "Unable to obtain exclusive access" in the Outreach log. SFDC briefly locks a record while any other user, integration, or trigger is touching it. Fix: stagger concurrent automations or configure Outreach to retry. Looks like a sync problem. It's a contention problem. The cure is upstream.

Verify, then document.
Have the user perform the activity in Outreach. Wait five minutes. Confirm in SFDC. Log the resolution in the team's troubleshooting tracker. The next person who hits this should find the answer before they have to ask.

Part three III. Where the AI earns its keep

The 30-day plan is realistic for one person
because of where AI compresses the work.

Six places, specifically.

Compression 01Interviews → taxonomy

Stakeholder interview synthesis.

Record every interview (with consent), transcribe, drop transcripts into Claude with a standard prompt: extract pain points, requested changes, systems mentioned, proper nouns. After 15 interviews I have an automatic taxonomy of what every team thinks is broken. No re-listening, no hand-coding. I'd build this as a reusable skill so the same framework applies to future interviews and QBRs.

Compression 02XML → English

Metadata documentation.

SFDC metadata exports are XML. Unreadable for humans. Pipe Flow XML, validation rules, and Apex through Claude with a template prompt: describe this automation in plain English, list the objects and fields it touches, flag anything that looks like dead code. Turns a week of writing into a day of reviewing AI drafts. I've run this exact pattern with Claude Code against SFDX metadata.

Compression 03Field clustering

Usage & consolidation analysis.

Export field usage, metadata, and a record sample to CSV. Ask Claude: for fields named Industry* on Account, which is most populated, which has the cleanest values, which should be the survivor. Claude doesn't make the final call. It clusters candidates faster than I can scroll through Workbench.

Compression 04Semantic matching

Semantic duplicate matching.

Native SFDC matching is exact-string-ish. Semantic matching ("Acme Inc" / "Acme Incorporated" / "ACME Holdings") is where AI earns its keep. I'd run a Clay or n8n workflow against the account list with an LLM as the similarity scorer. Output is a ranked merge-candidate list for human review.

Compression 05Hundreds → three

Outreach sync triage.

When the sync log has hundreds of errors, paste them into Claude and ask for the failure-mode taxonomy. Most "hundreds of errors" turn into "three patterns, here's the one fix for each." I haven't used Outreach, but this is the exact workflow I ran on Apollo.io sync logs at webAI. Same trick on Apex debug logs.

Compression 06Verbal → structured

Runbook drafting.

The runbook in Part II is the kind of doc I'd let Claude draft from a transcript of me walking through the diagnostic out loud, then edit. Verbal to structured doc is one of the highest-leverage AI uses for ops work. It lowers the friction enough that documentation actually gets written instead of perpetually deferred.

The principle ↘

AI doesn't replace the judgment calls. What to keep, what to deprecate, who to trust, what to fix first. It eliminates the grunt work between judgment calls, which is where ops debt usually piles up. A 30-day plan that would take a team of two manually is doable solo when synthesis, documentation, and data analysis are AI-accelerated.
